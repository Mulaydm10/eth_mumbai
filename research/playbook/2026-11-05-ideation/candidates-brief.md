# Candidate brief for validators (neutral descriptions; read-only input)
Context: an ETHGlobal Mumbai 2026 hackathon entry (Nov 5–7, 2026), team of 3, Android phones as the only hardware. Each candidate is described in its best version.

**S-A LIFEBOAT race.** A person whose private key has leaked watches sweeper bots steal any ETH they send in for gas, so they cannot rescue the tokens and approvals still in the wallet. LIFEBOAT uses one EIP-8141 *frame transaction* through Ethereum's new alternative mempool (ERC-8421): a sponsor or an ERC20 paymaster pays gas, and the transaction atomically revokes approvals and moves the assets to a fresh key, so no ETH ever touches the compromised address. It is proven against a sweeper bot the team writes. The adversarial twist: the same ERC20-paymaster feature lets *sweepers* drain tokens from compromised wallets with no ETH; the team measures how much value sits in known-compromised, gas-less wallets and proposes a paymaster/admission rule plus a public registry of sweeper delegate contracts (on Arkiv).

**S-B Tremor Witness.** Residents of old Mumbai buildings next to metro or flyover piling cannot prove how hard their walls shook, because the vibration meters belong to the contractor or authority. Phones on walls in several flats log peak particle velocity (PPV). They are time-aligned and checked by quorum (one human, one sensor) so a single faked phone is rejected, and they estimate the bearing to the source. Each confirmed event becomes a tamper-evident record (Arkiv entities with expiry; an ENSv2 name per building with role-based write permissions) that the contractor cannot edit or own.

**S-C Hum Clock.** Photo or video evidence (insurance damage claims, journalism, delivery proofs) can be back-dated or reused. The mains grid frequency wanders randomly around 50 Hz. A phone extracts that wander from a clip (mic hum, or the flicker of mains lights via the camera's rolling shutter) and matches it against a reference trace recorded by witness phones and published as a public ledger, which proves *when* the clip was recorded and reveals splices.

**S-D Pocket Sonar.** A phone's speaker emits an inaudible 18–22 kHz FMCW chirp and its mic turns the echoes into a breathing waveform (radar-style range FFT + CFAR), so breathing pauses (sleep apnoea, an elderly person living alone) are detected without a wearable. A decentralized-AI (0G) model makes the alarm decision at checkpoints and logs each decision; if the AI is unreachable, the device enters a declared safe state instead of faking a decision.

**E1 ReadLeak / LeakMeter.** A wallet's or DeFi app's RPC provider sees every address and token you look up (plus IP and timing), so it can link your wallets without any transaction. The tool replays a fixed corpus of wallet/DeFi reads through a normal RPC and through the Ethereum Foundation's Anon-RPC, shows what each provider could infer ("after 2 minutes it links these 3 wallets"), and publishes a before/after leak and compatibility score, with public chain/RPC registry data mirrored to Arkiv.

**E2 Fallback Checkout.** A stablecoin (USDT) checkout where the customer can still verify, reclaim (refund within a deadline) or complete the payment after the merchant's website and backend vanish: a static client reconstructs invoice state from the chain and ENSv2 names (merchant.fallback.eth, invoice-42.merchant.fallback.eth) and exercises the refund right. Overlaps: Unwind (onchain refunds), AnyAsset (ENS-branded stablecoin checkout), Enscribe (ENS invoices).

**E3 MachineLease (phone-only).** Equipment rental (generators, pumps, power tools, construction equipment) billed only for verified use: a phone clipped to the machine classifies running vs idle from its vibration/acoustic signature, an AI (0G) decides at checkpoints, metering stops if the AI is unreachable, and USDT settles by verified run-time with a refund of unused prepaid balance. Prior art noted by the team: IOFY (tokenized IoT rental sessions), Kleeto and MACH402 (metered rented computers), Paybot (robot control + gasless payment).

**E4 Hedged LP.** Ordinary Uniswap liquidity providers lose money versus holding (loss-versus-rebalancing). One-click hedging: as the position drifts toward the falling token, an agent places counter-orders (limit, stop, TWAP) through Definitive Flash advanced orders; the user sees "position vs holding, with and without the hedge", measured by replaying a real pool. Known prior art: Arrakis, Gamma, lpTOKEN.fun (auto-range), LP Agent (copy LP), Remora, Fables (dynamic fees), Delta Zero Hook (delta-neutral LP).
