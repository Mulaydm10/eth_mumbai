## The four profile questions
1. **What does a judge physically receive?** Two different things. (a) The finalist panel sees a **4-minute live demo plus 3 minutes of questions**, gated on a live link anyone can open, a 2–4 min video, a public repo with history and documented AI use. (b) Sponsor judges receive **the submission materials**: the write-up, the per-sponsor integration explanation and the video, read in under 2 minutes per project.
2. **Who judges, and against what?** (a) Finalist judges are a panel screening about 25 teams in 3 hours, scoring Technicality, Originality, Practicality, Usability (UI, UX, DX) and WOW factor, with no published weights. They react in minutes, and their first question is what inspired the project, i.e. whether the problem is real. (b) Each sponsor's judges are technical reviewers of their own product, looking for the deepest use of their newest feature, measured against their own track text.
3. **What separates the top few here?** From 1,680 scanned projects and 18 finalists, the winners had: one big original idea explained in one plain sentence, a Level-3 component (hard, working, with a measured number), a live and visible moment (5 of 16 finalists were physical builds), and a cited problem. Sponsors reward depth on their newest feature, and stacking only works when the sponsors depend on each other. 9 of 16 finalists won no sponsor prize: the two judge populations diverge.
4. **Where is the bottleneck for this human?** **Which idea** (a blank-ish page, with four existing candidates of uneven strength), then **making it defensible** to both judge populations at once. Shipping is the human's problem, not mine.

## Derived profile
- **Mode: both, DIVERGE then CONVERGE** (fixed by the human, and consistent with question 4). The passes are run in order and never blended.
- **This is a mixed event.** Finalist judging is an idea-plus-demo problem. I serve the **idea half**: which problem, which mechanism, which sponsor stack, which WOW moment and which measurable number. The **execution half** (building it, deploying it live, recording the video, delivering the pitch) is handed back to the team.
- **Selection dimensions, in order.** (1) Fit to the finalist rubric (all five criteria, unweighted). (2) Impact evidence: is the problem real and costly, with a citation? (3) Crowding against the hard filter and earlier winners. (4) Sponsor-stack fit and expected sponsor money. Novelty is never first.
- **Impact validation is central.** The finalist panel reacts to an idea in minutes and asks "what inspired this?" A cited problem is pitch material.
- **Out of scope for me:** building, deploying, the demo itself, the video, the pitch delivery, talking to sponsors, and judging feasibility against the team's clock.

## Triage: could one careful expert miss this in one pass?
**Yes.** The fan-out is justified for four reasons:
- **Wide design space.** 12 sponsors and about 41 tracks (roughly 30 From Scratch), crossed with phone sensors and the team's unusual RF/signal-processing skill.
- **Hidden crowding.** The crowding filter depends on about 1,680 earlier projects. A single pass reliably regresses to the crowded agent-spending idea; the compendium's own control test proves this ("Leash").
- **Coupled sponsor constraints.** Stacking only works under interdependence, at most 1 EF prize, Arc Studio and ENSv2 qualification rules, and the 0G hardware question.
- **Two diverging judge populations.** An idea optimised for one is invisibly weaker for the other.
