## FINDINGS

### F1 "Why a blockchain? A Google Sheet / WhatsApp video / signed PDF does it." Severity HIGH. Answered: PARTLY.
Evidence: C4 (07-converge-finalist-redteam.md, Q1) concedes the alternative is "a signed S3 log by a trusted notary" and that judges may say "a notarised PDF does it". VERDICT.md never states the answer; it only says Arkiv gives "tamper-evident" record. Real gap: the threat the chain defends against (the contractor deleting or editing the residents' record) is thin. Residents' own phones/Sheet are not controlled by the contractor, so the only party who can edit is the residents themselves; the chain adds value only against the residents' own society auditor or a later accusation "you forged it", which a timestamped signed file (OpenTimestamps, or a notarised PDF) also covers. Also a WhatsApp group of timestamped videos of cracking walls is closer to what courts accept today. [REASONING on the last sentence; the rest evidenced]. Confidence: medium-high that a judge asks it, high that the verdict lacks a crisp reply. Suggested crisp reply the team should rehearse: "the claim is multi-party consensus + public verifiability + no one party (including us and the society auditor) can silently rewrite; a PDF has one signer."

### F2 "A phone is not a seismograph." Severity HIGH. Answered: YES (well), with one residual.
Evidence: 08 T5 reframes to "trigger record" with spread/false-accept numbers; C3 simulation shows -25% to +17% PPV bias at 60 Hz cap, +30-60% from loose coupling. Residual: the MMRC quote ("cannot be that accurate", asianage 2017) is the problem the product is supposed to solve, and the product's own simulation confirms the objection for PPV. A trigger/agreement record cannot show a PPV against the (unfound) Indian limit, so the evidentiary payoff (limit exceeded) is not delivered. VERDICT admits "No Indian limit found". The honest answer is therefore "we prove an event happened, not that it was over a limit". That is a smaller claim than the pitch line "the only vibration record belongs to the contractor". Confidence high.

### F3 "Is ENS still forced after the redesign?" Severity HIGH. Answered: PARTLY.
Evidence: 08 T3 makes ENS the live sensor registry: a reading counts only if the signer resolves to a live subname. Better than before, but: (a) a registry of allowed signers is a ~20-line allowlist in the Arkiv write path or a society-owned Sheet; the verdict does not say why ENS beats an allowlist except vocabulary match ("expiring, revocable, non-transferable"); (b) verifying a signer's ENS record on every reading means the dashboard must resolve ENS at read time, which pulls ENS onto the critical path and in the kill-the-backend beat; (c) per-sensor key/resolver workaround is "probed, not exercised" (VERDICT Not covered) and is the single riskiest part for the biggest prize ($3k/$2k/$1k/$1k); (d) C4 notes ENSv2 already appeared at Tokyo finalist Kura (ENSv2 3rd), so it is not a novelty signal. So ENS is no longer decorative but is "load-bearing by design choice" rather than by necessity. Confidence medium-high.

### F4 "Would Arkiv judges see it as a log?" Severity HIGH. Answered: PARTLY.
Evidence: Arkiv rule (VERDICT, verbatim): "Not counted: ... Arkiv used only as a log that nothing reads." S-B writes readings and events; the dashboard reads them back. It passes the letter. But the product's core is append-only evidence, which is the most log-shaped use there is; the rubric's "at least one feature users see reads its data from Arkiv" is met by the dashboard only, and the quorum-by-query claim is the only part that is more than log. Risk is whether quorum is genuinely computed from Arkiv queries at read time (verdict says yes) versus computed client-side and stored. Also required files (friction.md, why-arkiv.md) need an honest "why not a DB" answer, same as F1. Burst write limits are untested (VERDICT Not covered): four phones at tens of Hz writing per-reading entities may hit limits; the plan presumably writes only confirmed events plus summaries, but the verdict says "each sensor reading ... is an Arkiv entity". Confidence medium.

### F5 CROPS "censorship resistance" claim real? Severity HIGH. Answered: PARTLY (the verdict hedges P but not C).
Evidence: threat model in the verdict is "contractor or authority cannot suppress or edit" the record. But the contractor never held the residents' phones, so suppression of capture was never possible; the realistic censorship vectors are (i) the dashboard host, (ii) the RPC/Arkiv endpoint operator, (iii) the society auditor who alone issues and revokes sensor subnames (a centralised kill-switch the demo celebrates: "auditor revokes one sensor live"). The auditor revocation is a feature for sybil control but is exactly a point of censorship: whoever controls the auditor key can exclude any flat's sensor. That contradicts C and "O (open)" and a CROPS judge can say it in one sentence. Also Arkiv is a testnet and "kill the backend" proves only that our server is not needed, not that the data is censorship-resistant against a hostile operator. ### F7 Is the stomp-on-a-table demo believable as piling vibration? Severity MED-HIGH. Answered: PARTLY.
Evidence: C4 beat sheet: heavy book dropped on a table 3 ft away; four phones all on the same table. C4's own Q4: "Physical collocation (all four on one table) is also easy to spoof by one actor." The central WOW "one phone shaken alone is rejected" proves the quorum rejects local shake, not that the system measures a distant source. Piling is low-frequency, ground-borne, through foundations at 5-30 Hz and over hours; a table thump is a broadband impulse. A sceptical judge sees: 4 phones agree because they share a table, one actor can defeat that, and a 60 Hz-capped browser sees the impulse not the spectrum. The verdict relies on "physical WOW" but the WOW is a different phenomenon from the claimed one. Mitigation is already partly planned (judges lend phones; labelled real replay), but the real-site replay is a recorded session [labelled], which a panel may discount. Confidence medium (judge reaction is [REASONING]; the table collocation fact is evidenced).
